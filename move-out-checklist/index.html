Move-out guide

Moving out of a Christchurch flat: the end-of-tenancy checklist

Everything you need to do when you leave a rental, in the order you'll do it — from giving notice to getting your bond back. Based on what Tenancy Services tells tenants.

Before you start

Start a few weeks out

Tenancy Services suggests landlords and tenants look over the place a few weeks before moving out, so there's time to fix anything before the final inspection. In a student flat, one person usually ends up organising it for everyone — it's easier if that's decided early.

This is general information

It isn't legal advice, and your tenancy agreement may add its own requirements. For the full rules, see Tenancy Services.

The checklist

Eight things to do when you move out

  1. 1

    Give notice the right way

    On a periodic tenancy, notice has to be in writing, and tenants generally need to give at least 21 days. On a fixed-term lease, check your notice requirements before the end date — ending a fixed term early generally needs your landlord's agreement.

  2. 2

    Sort the last rent and the bills

    Pay rent up to and including the last day of the tenancy, and settle anything else you owe for the property. Cancel the services in your name — power, gas, internet. If you pay for water, write down the meter reading on the final day, after you've moved out and cleaned.

  3. 3

    Get everything out

    All your belongings and all the rubbish have to go. Anything the landlord supplied — furniture, appliances — stays. If you made small changes, put things back to substantially the same condition unless your landlord has agreed they can stay.

  4. 4

    Clean to “reasonably clean and tidy”

    That's the standard Tenancy Services uses. A professional carpet clean is only needed if it's what it takes to meet that standard, and a clause in your agreement asking for more may not be enforceable.

  5. 5

    Book the final inspection

    Arrange a time with your landlord or property manager — most want to do it before they'll agree to a bond refund. Do it after everything is out and cleaned, ideally together. If you can't be there together, each do your own, and take photos.

  6. 6

    Hand back every key

    Return all the keys to the landlord.

  7. 7

    Confirm the bond refund

    The landlord submits the refund request in Bond Hub, and you get a notification to confirm the amounts — you'll need your tenant number (it looks like Tenant-12345678). Only accept the amount if you agree with it. If you don't, talk to your landlord first; if that doesn't fix it, Tenancy Services has mediation and the Tenancy Tribunal.

  8. 8

    Leave your contact details

    Give your landlord a way to reach you so mail and bond matters find you, and update your details with Tenancy Services. It's also the moment to ask for a reference for your next flat.
